   WHAT EACH IS MEANT TO LOOK LIKE (specular + shininess are what make them differ under the sun)
     sand       dry rippled sand: almost no highlight (0.05 / 4), the matt reference.
     brick      red brick with pale mortar: faint broad sheen (0.20 / 16).
     stone      grey cracked stone: medium highlight (0.45 / 48).
     crate      striped wood grain: matt (0.15 / 8); orange block on the minimap.
     metal      grid-lined steel pyramids: a sharp bright glint (1.0 / 256) - the clearest specular test.
     goal       flat bright blue with an emissive glow, so it is visible even in shade and fog.
     sandstone  yellow-tan brick (level 3 walls), matt.
     obsidian   near-black glassy pillars (level 3): tiny very sharp highlight (0.9 / 128).
     dune       pale dune sand (level 3 floor), very matt.
     ember      orange glowing braziers (level 3), emissive.

   GEOMETRY: vertices / indices per shape  (B2: every shape is drawn with glDrawElements)
     box       24 / 36    four vertices per face because each face needs its own normal AND texture coordinates
                          (8 corners would force one normal per corner and smooth the box into a ball under light)
     plane      4 /  6    one face, one normal: no splitting needed
     cylinder 102 / 288   24 segments: side 25 x 2 = 50 (the +1 column closes the texture seam), two caps 26 each
                          (a separate centre+ring so the cap normal is straight up/down, not the side normal)
     pyramid   16 / 18    4 side faces x 3 (each with its own face normal) + 4 for the base
     skybox     8 / 36    the ONLY shape that really uses 8 corners: skybox.frag uses the position as the direction
                          and ignores normals completely.
